# Sources and scope of the literature check

**Primary statement.** Sharareh Alipour and Amir Jafari, [*Balanced Fair Division and Generalized Kneser Graphs*, arXiv:2611.04486v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.04486v1), posted 3 November 2026. Theorems 1.1, 1.2 and 2.1, Lemmas 2.2 and 10.2. Theorems 1.1–1.2 guarantee nine people for any goods count; the chromatic number is exactly 14 on eight goods and between 10 and 14 inclusive on every even goods count at least eight. Section 2 gives the strict agent threshold, the dummy-good argument for odd counts, and the fact that the balanced sequence of chromatic numbers is nonincreasing. The open-problems paragraph explicitly asks for the value at half-sizes at least five.

**Definitions.** Membership in two nonempty groups is prescribed; every member receives their group's bundle. Goods are indivisible and all are allocated. Valuations are nonnegative, normalized at the empty set, and weakly increasing under inclusion. Additivity and strict preferences are not assumed. An agent assigned P accepts if some T contained in the other bundle Q has size at most two and u(P) ≥ u(Q minus T). T can depend on the agent. Bundle sizes differ by at most one. The allowance does not mean exchanging two goods or removing goods from one's own bundle.

**Previous ingredients.** [Kyropoulou, Suksompong and Voudouris](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2020.07.008) developed the graph approach for group allocations. [Balogh, Cherkashin and Kiselev](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.09394) give discrepancy-based upper colorings; the fourteen Fano-plane classes here are prior work. The [Ahlswede–Khachatrian Complete Intersection Theorem](https://www.math.uni-bielefeld.de/ahlswede/homepage/public/122.pdf) gives the largest single class, not a partition into such classes. The size calculation at half-sizes 4, 5 and 6 gives 5, 26 and 112 respectively. Johnson graph domination results concern covers by radius-one balls only; arbitrary independent families are allowed here. A graph with adjacency defined by intersection *exactly* two is also not this graph.

**Additional checks for the restricted statements.** [Moura (1999)](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcta.1998.2946) is prior work on enumerating maximal intersecting families; we do not claim the classification apparatus as new. A second arXiv/OpenAlex query, `discrepancy set systems few sets small matrices balanced colorings Fano plane`, gave mostly unrelated matches. A more relevant check is [Manurangsi–Suksompong on discrepancy and group fairness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01609), as well as the discrepancy references above. [Viswanathan–Mehta](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2024/pdfs/p2534.pdf) consider sparse additive preferences in individual allocation, not the prescribed two-group balance statement here. The two new arguments are offered as local derivations, not priority claims.
