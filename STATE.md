## What failed and why

- **Density alone does not settle the first open case.** For 252 five-sets the largest independent family has 26 vertices. Division and rounding give only ten colors. The gain came from checking *which* large families can cover the same layer, including the nonstandard ones. At eight goods the analogous density bound, 70/5=14, is already sharp.
- **The ten-good proof does not lift.** Balanced deletion maps from larger graphs to smaller ones. For a direct obstruction to reversing the map, label ten goods by the edges of a complete graph on vertices 0,…,4. For each i take the four edges incident to i plus the edge {i+1,i+2} (mod 5). Each pair of these five bundles shares exactly two goods: one common star edge and one cross incidence, since exactly one of j−i or i−j is 1 or 2 mod 5. They form a five-clique. No five-clique exists on twelve goods: encode a six-set by twelve ±1 coordinates. Two adjacent vertices have dot product ≤−4; five of them would give squared norm of their sum at most 5·12−2·10·4<0. Thus there cannot even be a homomorphism of the whole ten-good graph into the twelve-good one.
- **Fixing one large class and solving only a fractional cover cannot settle twelve goods.** For each five-set H of the twelve goods, take all six-sets meeting it in at least four places. Every vertex lies in 6·C(6,4)+C(6,5)=96 such classes. Giving each of C(12,5)=792 classes weight 1/96 covers every vertex for cost 33/4, less than nine. Restricting to the residual after fixing *any* first class still covers it for that cost. This is not an integer nine-class cover. An attempt at a shifted-family classification also did not supply an exclusion for ten arbitrary classes. We do not claim that shifting all classes preserves their union.
- **The symmetric counterexample template fails globally, not just on small goods counts.** Six support tests and both polarities, even with one arbitrary independent class added, cannot cover the graph; [proof](SIX-LISTS.md). Ten arbitrary one-sided triple tests cannot cover either; [proof](FOUR-GOOD-SUPPORT.md). This is not an exclusion of all asymmetric colorings.
- **Twelve versus thirteen colors.** The twelve-class exclusion is complete. A separate exploratory thirteen-slot run used the same large-family cutoff 14, max 85 seconds of search, and stopped by budget after 22,282,240 nodes with no low-demand frontier *in the visited part*. It is not an exclusion. Bounded threshold and recoloring searches likewise did not produce a full coloring; a partial coloring is not an upper-bound certificate. These exploratory runs are not part of the proof receipt.

**If work resumes:** prioritize excluding ten colors at twelve goods, or exhibit a validated ten-coloring at any even goods count. The first would extend only the finite guarantee to twelve goods, the second would disprove the universal guarantee. A useful next finite experiment is to certify **all unshifted** independent families with at least ceil(924/10)=93 members, then branch on their residual integer cover demands. Stop if a reduced-demand step needs unclassified families; do not promote the result of an incomplete branch or a fractional relaxation. More time on the same thirteen-color ten-good search cannot by itself answer the ten-person question.

## Feedback on the prompt and harness

1. **Name the two counts.** Suggested assignment: “For every number of goods and every prescribed split of ten people, prove balanced EF2 for arbitrary monotone valuations, or give one exact counterexample. If that remains open, find the strongest finite goods interval or all-goods preference class, and state which quantifier you restricted.” This prevents proving a ten-*good* theorem and delivering it as a ten-*person* theorem.
2. **State both certificate directions before naming a solver.** One ten-coloring disproves the universal claim; ruling out ten colors at one size is a finite positive result. An unspecified valid coloring is not necessarily an improvement, and the lower bound travels in the opposite direction from the one suggested by the original roadmap. Check the direction on adjacent sizes before spending a solver budget.
3. **Require either an exhaustiveness guard or a testable witness.** Record the largest omitted class (14 here), the least demand actually reached (15), and what would invalidate the proof. Keep the input and all checker-source hashes. An `unsat` report, an incomplete coloring and a timeout need distinct statuses. A different implementation is stronger evidence than rerunning the same executable.
4. **Use a private negative control, not a published number, for workflow comparisons.** The eight-good counting example and the candidate small-case explanations were declared before computing the ten-good cover, but workers could read a paper containing the eight-good answer. The resulting agreement/disagreement is a diagnostic, not a blinded model score. A better control would conceal a privately generated change to the intersection rule and require each arm to predict *both* the answer and which inference will fail.
