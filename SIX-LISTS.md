# Any number of goods: six approval lists can all be balanced within two

**Written proof, unrefereed.** Let each of at most six lists mark an arbitrary subset of the goods. There is a partition with bundle sizes differing by at most one in which the two counts on **each list** also differ by at most two. Thus any number of people using these lists are simultaneously EF2 in either orientation. A person may value a bundle by any weakly increasing function of its approved-item count, not necessarily the count itself. One additional person with a completely arbitrary monotone valuation can be accommodated by giving their prescribed group whichever bundle they weakly prefer. This does not cover ten arbitrary valuations.

**Proof.** Add one unapproved dummy if needed to make the goods count even, say 2n. Start with the vector all of whose entries are 1/2. In the unit cube fix its coordinate sum n and its sum on each of the six lists. The resulting nonempty bounded polytope has a vertex x. At most seven coordinates of x are strictly between zero and one: otherwise the corresponding columns of the seven equality constraints are linearly dependent, and small perturbations in both directions would contradict extremality. Fix all integral coordinates of x. On the remaining r ≤ 7 coordinates the required total t is an integer. Each residual list sum is a half-integer (integer multiples of 1/2 include integers). It suffices to round exactly t coordinates up, with absolute error strictly less than 3/2 on every list: twice each error is an integer and equals the absolute difference of the two list counts.

First try rounding up the t largest coordinates. The sum of all positive rounding errors equals the magnitude of the sum of all negative errors, namely t minus the sum of those t coordinates. Thus every list's absolute error is at most t(1−t/r), by averaging the t largest entries. If r=0 we are already done. For r ≤ 7 this bound is less than 3/2 except at (r,t)=(6,3),(7,3),(7,4). Complementing all residual coordinates and all rounding decisions identifies the last two cases without changing absolute errors.

* **Six coordinates, t=3.** Equality in the averaging bound requires all coordinates to equal 1/2. There are twenty triples to round up. Error ≥3/2 requires the list or its complement on those six coordinates to be exactly the chosen triple. A row rules out at most two choices; six rows cannot exhaust twenty.
* **Seven coordinates, t=3.** If the sum of the largest three is greater than 3/2 the first rounding works. Otherwise the sum of the largest four is at most 2 and that of the largest five is at most 5/2 (the remaining entries are each at most 1/2). Replace a residual list by its complement when necessary so its size is at most three; the total rounding error is zero, so this preserves absolute error. Empty and singleton lists never fail. A two-element list has sum at least 1/2 and strictly less than 3/2, hence its target is 1/2 or 1. It rules out at most the five triples containing the pair. A three-element list has target 1 or 3/2, since the complementary four coordinates have sum at most 2. The only possible failures are choosing the list itself or a triple disjoint from it: at most 1+4=5 choices. There are 35 triples; six lists rule out at most 30. A simultaneous rounding exists.

Remove any dummy. For an envious list user, removing the excess one or two approved goods from the other bundle suffices. This proves the economic statement without assuming transferable utility or identical valuations within a group.

**Sharpness for simultaneous list counts.** The seven lines of the Fano plane each have three points. Any two-coloring has a monochromatic line (the classical seven-triple obstruction). Thus one list has count discrepancy three. This is sharp for the *six-list discrepancy statement*, not a ten-person counterexample; putting a user of each of seven lists in both groups needs fourteen people. See [Balogh–Cherkashin–Kiselev](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.09394) for the known coloring construction.

**Restriction on counterexample searches.** For one support H, the two threshold classes require that the number of items of H in a balanced bundle exceeds, or falls short of, half of |H| by at least 3/2 (Frankl families). Pick any six supports. The partition just proved leaves both complementary bundles outside all twelve classes. These two vertices are adjacent, so even one additional arbitrary independent class cannot cover both. This rules out that specific 13-class construction at every even goods count with half-size at least three. It does not exclude asymmetric covers or arbitrary 13-colorings. The finite rounding counts are replayed in `check.py`; the reduction to at most seven coordinates is a written proof, not a machine formalization.

In particular, for every even number of goods at least eight, **exactly seven complementary pairs of these threshold classes are necessary and sufficient** when only paired support tests are permitted. Necessity is the six-list theorem; sufficiency uses seven Fano lines on seven of the goods, irrespective of the others. This is an exact answer for a restricted construction, not the unrestricted chromatic number.
