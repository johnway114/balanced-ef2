# A06 adversarial audit — unrefereed

**Outcome.** An independent in-session Python enumeration reproduced the exclusion of twelve colors at ten goods. This tests the mathematical result using a different search, not merely the same executable twice. It is AI-assisted internal verification, not external confirmation. The [harness receipt](RECEIPT.json) attests to the distributed checker; the independent worker's counts below are its reported results, not an additional archived receipt.

**Finite classification.** The reviewer independently obtained 70 common candidates for the anchored pair, and the anchored maximal-family histogram 7:36, 10:882, 12:1098, 14:900, 18:230, 21:2, 26:4. The four size-26, one triple-star and one opposite-star exceptions *above* 18 are all structured; at size 18 the exceptional orbits have 4,200, 4,200 and 25,200 labeled families. The auditor checked both relabeling the anchor and the small case with no pair meeting in exactly three goods. Counting only balls or assuming that an extremal-family theorem classifies every possible color would be unsound.

**Separate search.** In a putative twelve-cover at least one ball is needed: with no balls all classes have size at most 21, so twelve of size exactly 21 would have to be disjoint. Two triple stars are disjoint only for disjoint triples, so at most three such stars can be disjoint, and likewise at most three opposite stars. The auditor fixed a ball and independently searched the remainder in Python. It visited 402,041 states with no cover and no demand ≤14. Different counts from the release run are expected because the branch orders and root reduction differ. The reviewer also checked the delicate C++ rule: after selecting an exceptional column the group must become trivial; before that, retaining the entire selected structured orbit and ordering all tied exceptions after structured orbits preserves the stabilizer. The capacity bound counts actual columns, not just representatives.

**Economic implications checked.** The threshold uses strict inequality. A counterexample for fourteen people on ten goods must not be reported for thirteen. The dummy transfers existence to odd goods counts, not impossibility in the other direction. A worker initially missed the balanced-deletion result; inspection of §2 and Lemma 10.2 of the primary source corrected that. The direction is from a larger goods graph to a smaller one, so the finite lower bound is not universal.

**Other attacks and negative controls.** The proof and breaker workers got definitions, not the ledger or each other's predictions. Both expected density not to decide the ten-good case; one initially miscounted the ball size as 21 instead of 26 and corrected it. On the eight-good control the exact calculation is 70/5=14. The result is in the source supplied to workers; this is not a blind model benchmark. An analytic stability/overlap argument independently excluded ten colors, but the stronger bundled claim rests on the replayable complete search. An attempted twelve-good proof and a fractional-cover shortcut did not exclude ten colors there.

**Written proofs.** A separate proof worker checked the six-list vertex reduction, the strict 3/2 tolerance and both rounding cases. The check on seven fractional coordinates uses 35 triples, with at most five forbidden per row; the Fano obstruction is for *simultaneous counts*, not automatically for a ten-agent prescribed split. A fresh reviewer examined the four-good support proof: singleton ties and complementarities are allowed, but merely having four *positive singleton values* would not imply support on four goods. The Harris inequality is applied on independent signs within each polarity, not on the balanced slice. The hitting-set repair at eighteen goods and dummy deletion were also checked. No matching finite lower bound or four-supported theorem was located in the bounded searches; seek independent human review before any novelty claim.

**Stress case for the six-list proof.** A second reviewer supplied an actual seven-fractional-coordinate vertex where choosing an arbitrary nearest rounding fails. Use eight goods h, 1,…,6 and d; six lists are {h,i,j} for ij in {12,23,31,14,15,16}. The vertex x(d)=1, x(h)=3/4, x(i)=3/8 for i=1,…,6 preserves all six half-list sums and total four. The list equations and x(d)=1 fix it uniquely. Rounding up h,1,2 (in addition to d) misses the target on {h,1,2} by exactly 3/2; rounding up h,2,4 works. Thus the strict inequality and alternative-triple argument matter in a realizable instance. The reviewer also checked the sharp scope of the economic conclusions and identified the rounding precedents in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

## Pre-posting review: two separate tests

**Manuscript referee (not answer-blind).** A fresh AI reviewer received the README, both checker sources and the receipt, but not this audit or the research ledger. The referee statically reviewed both the cover search and its economic interpretation, did not execute the checker, and recommended a minor revision: state *monotone* in the headline, give the earlier Fano construction direct credit, and make the invariant on still-eligible columns explicit. All three changes were made in the README; no checker code changed.

**The wording matters.** Without monotonicity even two people can fail. Take ten goods, one distinguished, and give both people this same valuation: all sets of size three or four have value one; a five-set has value one exactly when it contains the distinguished good; other sets have value zero. In every balanced split the person without the distinguished good gets zero, while the opposing bundle remains worth one after any zero, one or two deletions. This is a counterexample to “arbitrary preferences,” not to the corrected theorem.

**Answer-blind reconstruction (not a human reviewer).** A separate fresh worker received only this question: cover the five-subsets of a ten-element set by as few families as possible, requiring intersection at least three within each family. It did not receive the sought value, application, paper, manuscript, code, prior reviews or receipt. Its transcript records arithmetic in its own Python and JavaScript kernels and no outside lookups. The blindness was to this project's result, not a claim about model training data.

Its frozen interval was **[12,14]**, not the submitted **[13,14]**. It enumerated maximal families through one vertex (rather than a pair), classified the sizes 21 and 26, and used ball-overlap budgets to exclude all covers with eleven families. It also found a fourteen-ball cover. Its centers, with T denoting 10, were `1239T 12467 12589 13456 13789 1457T 1468T 23468 23579 2478T 2569T 3458T 3679T 56789`; the worker counted 172 vertices covered once, 48 twice and 32 three times. Its twelve-ball search timed out. It neither reproduced nor contradicted the decisive exclusion of twelve *arbitrary* families. In people rather than colors, the answer-blind result recovers an eleven-person guarantee, one short of twelve.

**Posting judgment.** The corrected finite theorem is suitable for an explicitly unrefereed research note with the checker, if the author chooses to share it. Do not describe it as human-reviewed, as completely reproduced by the answer-blind arm, or as an all-goods theorem. The previous implementation that reproduced the full exclusion knew what it was trying to verify. The next useful external review is of that last exclusion, not the fourteen-class construction.
